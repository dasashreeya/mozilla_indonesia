# CPU viability tests: what is actually supported

Measured locally on 2026-09-06. No paid GPU jobs or ASR training were launched.

## Direct answer

**The evidence does not support assuring a 0.04-0.09 local WER improvement,
or a public score below 0.20.** Those numbers must not be treated as an
expected return from the proposed experiments.

The CPU tests demonstrate a much smaller improvement: approximately
**0.0054 absolute local WER** from a three-Qwen contextual selector.
This is an exploratory result on existing predictions, not a new public
score or a guarantee of performance on unseen conversations.

## What was run

- Verified 509 validation clips, 22,453 reference words, nine complete
  prediction files, prepared-manifest hashes and Jember session separation.
- Evaluated 37 voting configurations, including insertion-aware voting.
- Fitted a small contextual selector for three different model pools.
  It uses model agreement, KEEP, candidate length and word/bigram counts
  from the original training transcripts. It does not listen to audio.
- Used five folds of entire Jember sessions. For each fold, fitting or
  configuration selection excluded its evaluation sessions. The complete
  development conversation was evaluated with settings learned from Jember
  only; its references were not used to fit or select the methods.
- Re-scored every emitted transcript with the official-normalizer WER
  implementation. Ran 5,000 session-bootstrap draws per practical method.
- Recomputed whole-clip and word-selection oracles and a narrow oracle for
  seven commonly cited variant groups.
- Decoded and measured all 215 prepared Jember validation audio windows
  using CPU/ffmpeg, covering the 20 held-out sessions.

The main experiment took about 9.5 seconds; the prepared-audio measurements
took about 4.4 seconds on this machine. These timings exclude development
of the diagnostics and do not measure ASR inference.

## Practical results

These are emitted predictions, with fitting/selection separated by group.
The baseline is the earlier saved fine-tune at 0.2685, rather than the
separate same-machine control at 0.2697.

| Method | Mixed WER | Jember WER | Dev WER |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Saved Qwen baseline | 0.268472 | 0.336814 | 0.226649 |
| Group-selected voting | 0.263484 | 0.336462 | 0.218824 |
| Fixed three-Qwen insertion-aware vote | 0.267537 | 0.342562 | 0.221624 |
| Contextual selector: three Qwen | **0.263083** | **0.329188** | **0.222629** |
| Contextual selector: three Qwen + Whisper | 0.262816 | 0.330009 | 0.221696 |
| Contextual selector: one Qwen + Whisper | 0.268472 | 0.336814 | 0.226649 |

The three-Qwen selector uses the saved fine-tune, `arm-combo` and
`arm-kitchen`. It produces **121 fewer edit errors**, from 6,028 to 5,907.
It improves 123 clips and worsens 33; the remaining clips tie in edit count.
At the Jember session level, 16 improve, four tie and none worsen.

Its Jember delta is -0.00763. The conditional session-bootstrap interval
is approximately [-0.01013, -0.00536]. This is encouraging exploratory
evidence, not an independent confirmation: folds share training groups,
the holdout has been inspected in earlier work, speaker identities are
incomplete, and development contains only one conversation. A fresh split
is still needed before relying on the gain.

This tests one small selector, not the full potential of contextual or
generative correction. The single-Qwen-plus-Whisper selector conservatively
retained the baseline's scored words; that outcome does not rule out every
possible way to combine those models.

## Optimistic diagnostics are much better than actual selection

The following methods use the correct reference to make perfect choices.
They cannot be submitted as systems.

- Perfect choice of one complete transcript among nine: **0.231417**.
- Perfect word recombination among three Qwen: **0.226696**.
- Perfect word recombination among three Qwen + Whisper: **0.188215**.
- Perfect word recombination among all nine: **0.179620**.
- Perfect selection among the seven listed variant groups: **0.259653**.
- All-nine word oracle plus those variant alternatives: **0.173073**.

The seven groups are ning/neng/nang, nggak/gak, kaya/kayak, karna/karena,
iki/ki, kandhang/kandang and lek/nek. Even perfect choices among these
specific groups remove only 198 baseline errors. This is not a bound on
all spelling or convention errors.

The combined 0.173073 oracle already accounts for overlap between word
recombination and those variant corrections; their gains cannot simply
be added. The lattice uses one fixed alignment and observed insertion
sequences. It is not a universal bound on other alignments or new text
generated by a better model.

## How far the working target remains

At 22,453 words, WER at most 0.148 permits at most 3,323 errors.

- Baseline errors: **6,028**.
- Required net reduction: **2,705**.
- Three-Qwen selector's measured reduction: **121**, about 4.5% of that gap.
- Remaining reduction after that selector: **2,584**.
- Even the all-nine-plus-variants oracle retains **3,886** errors, 563
  above this scenario's allowance.

Therefore the current CPU results do not justify predicting 0.148 from
recombination of these hypotheses. They also do not prove that a new ASR
model or a more capable corrector cannot reach it.

The 0.148 local target remains a planning scenario. The previously claimed
constant local-to-public offset was not calibrated using matched models;
these results do not establish a future public score or leaderboard rank.

## Does the noise hypothesis survive direct audio measurement?

Yes, as an experimental lead. Across the 20 sessions, using medians measured
from the actual prepared validation windows:

- Estimated SNR versus WER: Spearman **-0.642**.
- Excluding the problematic session 35: **-0.582**.
- Leaving out any single session: between **-0.747 and -0.582**.
- RMS volume versus WER: **+0.110**.
- Partial rank association controlling silence, OOV and mean duration:
  approximately **-0.480**; exploratory with a small sample.

However, this SNR estimate is loud-frame versus quiet-frame energy contrast,
not a measured separation of speech and noise. A synthetic check confirms
the limitation: halving amplitude leaves the estimate unchanged, while
adding silence raises it sharply without removing noise from the original
signal. Silence and speaking patterns can affect the proxy.

Even an unrealistic scenario that perfectly transcribes the five sessions
with the lowest estimated SNR would leave mixed WER at **0.20995**.
That scenario is not a forecast or an upper bound on augmentation, which
could also affect other sessions and the development slice.

No new ASR predictions were generated from altered audio. CPU acoustic
measurements cannot supply the missing augmentation effect size.

## Inference budget implications

Using the reported 23-minute full-test Qwen runtime, three Qwen models would
take approximately 69 minutes before additional overhead. This makes that
pool a plausible candidate for an offline benchmark, not a verified package.

Adding Whisper buys only six additional net errors in the small selector
comparison. The saved 509-clip Whisper runtime extrapolates to about 84
minutes for 2,118 clips. Combining that estimate with three 23-minute Qwen
passes gives about 153 minutes before overhead, exceeding two hours.

Those measurements come from different execution configurations. They are
screening arithmetic, not a controlled end-to-end benchmark. There is no
reason yet to pay that extra inference cost for the measured six errors.
VibeVoice accuracy and runtime remain completely unmeasured in this work.

## Decision after these tests

1. Retain the three-Qwen selector as a modest candidate for independent
   confirmation. It is not evidence of a large breakthrough.
2. Keep alignment inspection and one controlled noise/channel training
   comparison as justified next experiments. Their gains remain unknown.
3. Run a capped VibeVoice benchmark to measure whether it supplies new
   correct alternatives at an acceptable runtime.
4. Test OpenSLR followed by target adaptation as a separate training
   hypothesis. These CPU results say nothing about its effect size.
5. Expand investment only after actual training/decoding results show a
   repeatable improvement. A score guarantee cannot be supplied in advance.

## Reproduce and inspect

From the `mozilla_indonesia` repository root:

```sh
python3 -m unittest tests.test_cpu_viability -v
python3 scripts/cpu_viability.py
python3 scripts/cpu_audio_viability.py
```

Six audit tests pass, including 250 random exhaustive-path comparisons,
official normalization checks, insertion voting, group separation and
checks that held-out labels cannot change fitted weights or features.
